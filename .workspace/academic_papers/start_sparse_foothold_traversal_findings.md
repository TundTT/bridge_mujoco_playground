# START: Traversing Sparse Footholds with Terrain Reconstruction

**Authors:** Ruiqi Yu, Qianshi Wang, Hongyi Li, Zheng Jun, Zhicheng Wang, Jun Wu, Qiuguo Zhu
**Year:** 2025 (IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, accepted December 2025; arXiv:2512.13153)

## Summary

START is a single-stage end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that trains a quadruped robot to traverse highly sparse, randomized footholds (stepping stones, balance beams as narrow as 0.15m, stepping beams, and gaps) using only onboard egocentric depth vision and proprioception. The core insight is that using an explicit local heightmap — reconstructed in real time by a learned Terrain Reconstruction Network (TR-Net) — as an intermediate representation dramatically outperforms implicit terrain encoding, because it preserves precise geometric cues (edges, safe foothold centres) that the policy needs for exact foot placement. The full system achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a DEEP Robotics Lite3 quadruped at speeds up to 1.5 m/s across diverse real-world sparse terrains.

## Findings Relevant to the 0.1m Bridge Problem

- **Balance beams as narrow as 0.15m are solved with ~80–100% success rate.** The DEEP Robotics Lite3 robot has a nominal stance wider than 0.15m; START achieves this by learning an inverted-triangle stance — it aligns its body over the beam centre and shifts legs into a dynamically stable configuration — rather than requiring all four feet to be simultaneously placed on the surface at standard spacing. This is directly analogous to our 0.1m barrier: the policy found a non-standard gait that places feet sequentially and asymmetrically.

- **Explicit heightmap > implicit encoding for extreme narrowness.** Ablation shows that the PIE baseline (implicit terrain encoding via latent vectors, analogous to our current setup) collapses to a single averaged gait and cannot adapt foot placement on narrow beams. Replacing it with an explicit reconstructed heightmap (5 cm/cell, 0.8m wide, 0.5–1.1m in front of the robot) gives the policy a clear geometric signal about beam edges and safe centre regions. At the narrowest beams (0.175m width), START with explicit heightmap achieves 100% success; PIE drops to ~40%. Our 13×13 binary heightmap at 3 cm/cell is spatially comparable in resolution, but the binary encoding discards height gradient and edge sharpness — switching to a continuous (float) heightmap and expanding it to cover beneath and behind the robot (not just in front) may provide the edge-precision signal needed at 0.1m.

- **Foot-edge penalty is critical for precise placement.** START adds an explicit reward term r_feetedge that penalises feet landing within 2.5 cm, 3.5 cm, or 5.0 cm of a terrain edge (penalty weights 1.0, 0.5 per zone). Removing this term ("START w/o FtEdge") causes the robot to frequently step on beam edges, leading to instability and high mean edge violation (MEV). This is a concrete, cheap reward addition we have not tried: penalise foot contact positions that fall within N cm of the bridge edge, measured from the heightmap.

- **Terrain progressive curriculum is essential; training directly on hard tasks fails completely.** The ablation "START w/o TerProg" (training on all terrain difficulties from scratch) results in near-zero success on challenging terrains. START's curriculum starts on low-randomness stepping stones, then progressively introduces balance beams and stepping beams. For our problem this validates the existing 0.8m → 0.1m width curriculum direction, but suggests we may need finer granularity and explicit easy-terrain "scaffolding" at the transition into sub-stance-width bridges.

- **Adaptive Sampling (AdaSmpl) accelerates exploration in hard terrain.** AdaSmpl monitors the coefficient of variation (CV) of episode reward: when CV is high (unstable policy), it probabilistically replaces reconstructed heightmap inputs with ground-truth heightmap, giving the policy a clean signal to bootstrap from. As training stabilises, it gradually reduces ground-truth injection. For our 0.1m stage — where the policy currently gets zero reward and no learning signal — injecting ground-truth heightmap early in training could bootstrap exploration where the reconstructed (or binary) map is too noisy.

- **Gait plasticity, not gait rigidity, is the mechanism.** Figure 9(e) shows the robot on 0.175m balance beams uses a distinct inverted-triangle leg arrangement, actively re-configuring stance width mid-traverse. This emerges without any explicit gait constraint — just the foot-edge penalty and accurate terrain perception together force the policy to discover alternative foot placements. This suggests that removing the air-time gait constraint (which we already did) is a necessary but not sufficient condition; what is additionally needed is the geometric edge signal that makes narrow-beam foot placement rewarding.

- **Heightmap covers body + rear region, not just forward look.** TR-Net reconstructs a heightmap covering areas beneath AND behind the robot (via GRU temporal memory), not only the forward-looking window. This means the policy can assess current footing stability, not just plan future steps. Our current 13×13 map is centred ahead of the robot; adding a body-centric component (which body parts are currently over the bridge surface) could help the robot understand its own support polygon relative to the 0.1m bridge.

- **Beam widths tested: 0.4m, 0.3m, 0.25m, 0.2m, 0.175m** (with 0.275m gaps alongside). At 0.175m width with maximum randomisation START achieves ~80% success rate in simulation and 100% in real-world lab tests. The 0.15m beam width is also shown in figure labels, and the robot handles it at reduced but nonzero success. This demonstrates the barrier we face at 0.1m (~1/3 of stance width) is not categorically different from what START overcomes at 0.15m (~half of stance width) — it is a matter of degree.

## Recommendation

The highest-priority change to try is replacing the binary heightmap with a continuous float heightmap and adding an explicit foot-edge penalty reward (r_feetedge) that penalises foot contacts within ~3–5 cm of the bridge edge — this is the single intervention that START's ablations most clearly identify as necessary for precise placement on sub-stance-width surfaces. As a second step, adopt AdaSmpl-style ground-truth heightmap injection during the 0.1m curriculum stage to bootstrap exploration when the policy is stuck at zero reward.
